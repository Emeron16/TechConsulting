# SOP-301: CAPA Effectiveness Check Procedure

## 1. Purpose

Defines how Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPAs) are drafted, approved,
implemented, and verified for effectiveness.

## 2. CAPA Plan Requirements

Every CAPA plan must include:

1. A statement of the root cause it addresses (must trace back to a
   deviation investigation's documented root cause, per SOP-220)
2. Specific, measurable corrective action(s) — vague actions such as
   "retrain staff" without a defined training plan and verification method
   are not acceptable
3. A preventive action addressing systemic recurrence, where applicable
4. An effectiveness check plan: what metric or observation will confirm the
   CAPA worked, and over what monitoring period

## 3. Effectiveness Check Timing

- Standard CAPAs: effectiveness verified no earlier than 90 days and no
  later than 180 days after implementation.
- CAPAs tied to a Critical deviation (per SOP-114): effectiveness verified
  no earlier than 60 days and no later than 120 days after implementation,
  given the elevated risk profile.

## 4. Effectiveness Determination

A CAPA is considered **effective** if, during the monitoring period, no
recurrence of the same root-cause category (per SOP-220 Section 3) occurs
for the affected process or product line. A CAPA is considered
**ineffective** if a recurrence is observed — in that case a new CAPA must
be opened referencing the original as related, and the original CAPA record
remains open with a documented ineffectiveness note rather than being closed.

## 5. Approval Authority

- CAPA plans for deviations classified Critical require Site Quality Head
  approval before implementation.
- CAPA plans for deviations classified Major require Quality Reviewer/CAPA
  Owner approval.
- Effectiveness check closure requires Quality Reviewer sign-off in all
  cases; Critical-linked CAPAs additionally require QP acknowledgment.
